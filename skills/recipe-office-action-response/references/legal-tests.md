# Office action analysis: the legal tests

Each test names its source. Fetch it with `flowleap-legal` and quote it when the analysis turns on it. An item marked "check" is not confirmed: write it for the attorney as a check, not as law.

## Search report categories (EPO)

The search report gives the searcher's view of the searched claims. It is not an objection of the examining division.

| Category | Meaning (Guidelines B-X 9.2) | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| X | The document alone shows that the claim is not novel or not inventive | Do not choose between novelty and inventive step. Run both tests and say which one the text supports |
| Y | Inventive step, with another Y document of the same category | Run the inventive-step test on the combination |
| A | Background; general state of the art | No objection. Read it only when it helps the comparison |
| P | Published between the priority date and the filing date | A date issue: depends on whether the priority claim is valid for the claim |
| E | An earlier-filed document published on or after the filing date | Potential Art. 54(3) art: novelty only, not inventive step |
| D | Cited in the application | Read the application's statement about it |
| L | Cited for another reason (for example a doubt on priority) | Say the reason the report gives |
| T | Theory or principle behind the invention | Background |

Write "Source of the objection: search report categories (the search opinion text was not read)" in each block that you infer from categories.

## Date check

1. Find the effective filing date of each claim. A priority claim counts for a claim only when the priority document supports that claim. If you cannot read the priority document, say so.
2. US (35 U.S.C. 102): a printed publication before that date is 102(a)(1) art. A US patent or US published application that was effectively filed before that date is 102(a)(2) art even if it was published later; 102(d) dates it back to its earliest priority application that describes the subject-matter. Check the 102(b) exceptions (grace period disclosures, common ownership).
3. EP: a document published before the date of filing or priority is Art. 54(2) art. A European application with an earlier date that was published on or after that date is Art. 54(3) art, for novelty only.
4. Never write that a date is a "complete answer". Write "date issue for the attorney" and the facts.

## Claim interpretation

- USPTO: the broadest reasonable interpretation in light of the specification (MPEP 2111). A means-plus-function term is read under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) (MPEP 2181).
- EPO: read the claim with its ordinary meaning to the skilled person (Guidelines F-IV 4). Check: G 1/24 (2025) on the use of the description and drawings when claims are interpreted; the attorney confirms.
- Quote the definitions in the application that you rely on. Mark each disputed term for the attorney.

## Novelty

- US: each element "arranged as required by the claim" in a single reference (MPEP 2131). Inherency only when the feature necessarily flows from the reference; the examiner must give a basis in fact or technical reasoning (MPEP 2112).
- EP: the subject-matter must be "directly and unambiguously derivable" from the document, including what is implicit to the skilled person (Guidelines G-VI).
- An element table can mark each element "disclosed" and still miss the arrangement. Check the arrangement in a separate line.

## Inventive step and obviousness

EP, problem-solution approach (Guidelines G-VII 5.2), one block per independent claim:

1. The closest prior art, and why it is the closest.
2. The distinguishing features.
3. Their technical effect, with the passage of the application that shows it.
4. The objective technical problem, with no pointer to the solution.
5. Could-would: would the skilled person, with the teaching of the other documents, have arrived at the claimed solution? Quote the passage that would lead there, or say that none was found.

US, Graham and KSR (MPEP 2141 to 2145, 716):

1. The Graham findings: scope and content of the art, the differences, the level of ordinary skill.
2. The KSR rationale that the examiner named (MPEP 2143, A to G), and whether the action gives "some articulated reasoning with some rational underpinning".
3. The reason to combine, the reasonable expectation of success, teaching away, and secondary considerations (MPEP 716).

## Amendments

- EP: Rule 137(4) requires the applicant to identify the amendments and indicate their basis in the application as filed. Test each amendment against "directly and unambiguously derivable" (Art. 123(2)) and against intermediate generalisation: a feature taken from one embodiment without the features it is linked to (Guidelines H-V 3.2.1). Rule 137(5): no claims to unsearched subject-matter that does not combine with the searched invention.
- US: written description and support under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) (MPEP 2163); no new matter (35 U.S.C. 132).
- Scope effects. US: an amendment and an argument can each narrow the claims (prosecution history estoppel and argument-based disclaimer). EP: write "effect on scope, for the attorney"; check whether national courts use the file history.
- Which feature to add is the attorney's choice. List the candidates; do not rank them as the answer.

## Reply completeness (US)

A reply must address "every ground of objection and rejection", point out "the specific distinctions", and "show how the amendments avoid such references" (37 CFR 1.111(b), (c)). For each objection, list what a reply must address. Also check whether the examiner designated the parts of a complex reference relied on (37 CFR 1.104(c)(2)); a missing designation is a procedural point for the attorney.

## Duty of disclosure (US)

Art cited against a family member can be material. Check 37 CFR 1.56, and list counterpart art that is not in the US record.
